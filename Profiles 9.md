COUNTRY: Andorra
TITLE: Andorra — Christianity & Persecution Profile
SLUG: country-profiles/andorra
META DESCRIPTION: Andorra is a Catholic co-principality with full religious freedom. One of its two heads of state is a Catholic bishop.
CATEGORY: Country Profiles
TAGS: Andorra, Europe, Catholic, Co-Principality, Religious Freedom
PERSECUTION TIER: Lower

## Overview

Andorra is around 85 percent Catholic and holds an arrangement found nowhere else. Its two co-princes are the Bishop of Urgell in Spain and the President of France, a system dating to 1278. The 1993 constitution guarantees religious freedom and gives the Catholic Church a recognized special relationship. There is no persecution.

Minority communities, including a Muslim population among immigrant workers, have sought a dedicated place of worship for years without securing one, which is the main religious freedom question in the country. Christians practice without restriction.

## Key Facts

- **Persecution Score:** Lower (5/100), **Rank:** #152 of 192 countries
- **Estimated Christian Population:** about 85 percent
- **Current Status:** Full religious freedom
- **Notable:** A Catholic bishop serves as one of two heads of state

COUNTRY: Australia
TITLE: Australia — Christianity & Persecution Profile
SLUG: country-profiles/australia
META DESCRIPTION: Australia has full religious freedom and a Christian population that fell below half at the 2021 census. Debate centers on religious discrimination law.
CATEGORY: Country Profiles
TAGS: Australia, Oceania, Catholic, Anglican, Secularization, Religious Discrimination, Royal Commission
PERSECUTION TIER: Lower

## Overview

Australia recorded 44 percent Christian at the 2021 census, down from 61 percent in 2011, with 39 percent reporting no religion. Catholics are the largest single group. Religious freedom is protected by section 116 of the constitution and by state law, and there is no persecution.

The live issue is how religious bodies are treated under anti-discrimination law. A federal Religious Discrimination Bill failed in 2022 and later attempts stalled. Faith-based schools want to preserve the right to hire staff who share their beliefs, and state laws in Victoria and elsewhere have narrowed that right. Conscience protections for doctors and pharmacists in relation to assisted dying and abortion vary by state.

The Royal Commission into Institutional Responses to Child Sexual Abuse, which reported in 2017, found extensive abuse in Catholic, Anglican, and other church institutions. Cardinal George Pell was convicted in 2018 and acquitted unanimously by the High Court in 2020.

A bishop and a priest were stabbed during a livestreamed service at a Sydney Assyrian church in April 2024 by a teenager, an attack police declared terrorism.

## Key Facts

- **Persecution Score:** Lower (7/100), **Rank:** #118 of 192 countries
- **Estimated Christian Population:** about 44 percent by the 2021 census
- **Current Status:** Full religious freedom
- **Live Issue:** Religious discrimination law and faith-based school employment

### Sources & References

- Australian Bureau of Statistics, *2021 Census, religion* — abs.gov.au

CATEGORY: Country Profiles
TAGS: Austria, Central Europe, Catholic, Church Tax, Secularization, Religious Freedom
PERSECUTION TIER: Lower

## Overview

Austria is around 52 percent Catholic by membership, down from over 70 percent two decades ago, with Protestant and Orthodox minorities and a growing non-religious share. The state recognizes religious communities as public bodies and collects church contributions for them. Religious freedom is complete and there is no persecution.

Tens of thousands leave the Catholic Church each year, partly to avoid the church contribution and partly after abuse revelations. The 2015 Islam Law restricted foreign funding of mosques and imams, applying rules not placed on churches, and drew criticism for unequal treatment. Jihadist attacks, including the Vienna shooting of November 2020, have raised security around places of worship of all faiths.

## Key Facts

- **Persecution Score:** Lower (6/100), **Rank:** #131 of 192 countries
- **Estimated Christian Population:** about 60 percent including Protestant and Orthodox
- **Current Status:** Full religious freedom

COUNTRY: Kosovo
TITLE: Kosovo — Christianity & Persecution Profile
SLUG: country-profiles/kosovo
META DESCRIPTION: Kosovo is predominantly Muslim with a small Serbian Orthodox and Albanian Catholic Christian minority. Orthodox heritage sites need protection, and the north remains tense.
CATEGORY: Country Profiles
TAGS: Kosovo, Balkans, Serbian Orthodox, Albanian Catholic, Heritage Sites, Decani, Religious Freedom
PERSECUTION TIER: Lower

## Overview

Kosovo is around 95 percent Muslim, most of them ethnic Albanians who practice a moderate and largely secular Islam. Christians include Serbian Orthodox believers, concentrated in the north and in enclaves, and Albanian Catholics, whose community has deep roots. The constitution establishes a secular state and protects religious freedom.

The main religious concern is the security of Serbian Orthodox heritage. Visoki Dečani monastery, the Patriarchate of Peć, and other medieval sites are UNESCO World Heritage properties listed as in danger. The March 2004 riots damaged or destroyed dozens of Orthodox churches and monasteries. Dečani remains under NATO KFOR protection. Kosovo's Constitutional Court confirmed Dečani's ownership of disputed land in 2016, and the government did not implement the ruling for years, a failure raised repeatedly by the EU and the U.S.

The north, where Serbs are the majority, saw an armed attack at Banjska monastery in September 2023 in which a Kosovo police officer and three attackers were killed, which raised tension around Orthodox sites further. Albanian Catholics, who include the family of Mother Teresa, face no restrictions.

## Key Facts

- **Persecution Score:** Lower (16/100), **Rank:** #74 of 192 countries
- **Estimated Christian Population:** about 4 percent, Serbian Orthodox and Albanian Catholic
- **Primary Concern:** Protection of Serbian Orthodox heritage sites and Serbian communities
- **Current Status:** Religious freedom in law, heightened tension in the north

### Sources & References

COUNTRY: Kuwait
TITLE: Kuwait — Christianity & Persecution Profile
SLUG: country-profiles/kuwait
META DESCRIPTION: Kuwait permits several recognized churches and a small indigenous Christian community, while forbidding evangelism and giving Kuwaiti converts from Islam no protection.
CATEGORY: Country Profiles
TAGS: Kuwait, Gulf States, Expat Christians, Indigenous Christians, Converts, Religious Restrictions
PERSECUTION TIER: Medium

## Overview

Kuwait is around 75 percent Muslim among its total population, with Christians near 18 percent, almost all expatriate. Kuwait is unusual in the Gulf because it has a small indigenous Christian community, a few hundred citizens whose families were naturalized decades ago, mostly of Arab Christian origin. Several recognized churches operate, including Catholic, Anglican, Coptic, Greek Orthodox, Armenian, and the National Evangelical Church. Many other congregations meet in shared or rented space without legal recognition.

Evangelizing Muslims is prohibited. A Kuwaiti Muslim who converts faces personal status consequences through sharia courts, including loss of marriage validity and inheritance, and severe family pressure. Building new churches is effectively blocked, and existing churches are overcrowded. Kuwait moved to advisory level 3 following the onset of hostilities between the United States and Iran in February 2026, with ongoing drone and missile threats.

## Key Facts

- **Persecution Score:** Medium (31/100), **Rank:** #53 of 192 countries
- **Estimated Christian Population:** about 18 percent, almost entirely expatriate, with a few hundred citizens
- **Primary Threats:** No protection for Kuwaiti converts, evangelism ban, no new church construction
- **Open Doors WWL:** Not ranked in the top 50

### Sources & References

- U.S. Department of State, *Report on International Religious Freedom: Kuwait* (most recent edition) — state.gov

COUNTRY: Kyrgyzstan
TITLE: Kyrgyzstan — Christianity & Persecution Profile
SLUG: country-profiles/kyrgyzstan
META DESCRIPTION: Kyrgyzstan tightened its religion law in 2025, and Kyrgyz converts from Islam face family violence and burial disputes.
CATEGORY: Country Profiles
TAGS: Kyrgyzstan, Central Asia, Religion Law, Registration, Converts, Burial Disputes, WWL Top 50
PERSECUTION TIER: Medium

## Overview

Kyrgyzstan is around 90 percent Muslim with Christians near 7 percent, mostly Russian Orthodox, with small Protestant and Catholic communities that include ethnic Kyrgyz converts. It was once the most open state in Central Asia and has become more restrictive. Open Doors ranked Kyrgyzstan 40th on the 2026 World Watch List.

A new religion law signed in early 2025 raised the membership threshold for registering a religious organization from 200 to 500 founders, banned unregistered activity, restricted religious education and literature, and limited missionary work. Many small Protestant congregations cannot meet the threshold, which leaves them illegal.

Kyrgyz converts face family and village pressure, including beatings, expulsion from home, and refusal of burial in village cemeteries, which has left some families unable to bury their dead. Local officials frequently side with the community.

## Key Facts

- **Persecution Score:** Medium (36/100), **Rank:** #48 of 192 countries
- **Estimated Christian Population:** about 7 percent
- **Primary Threats:** The 2025 religion law, family and village pressure on converts, burial refusals
- **Open Doors WWL 2026:** #40

### Sources & References

## Overview

San Marino is around 90 percent Catholic and traces its founding to Saint Marinus, a Christian stonemason said to have fled Diocletian's persecution in the fourth century. Religious freedom is protected and there is no persecution. A 2021 referendum legalized abortion, against the position of the church.

## Overview

Solomon Islands is over 95 percent Christian, with the Anglican Church of Melanesia, Catholics, the South Seas Evangelical Church, and Seventh-day Adventists the largest bodies. Religious freedom is protected and there is no persecution.

During the ethnic tensions of 1998 to 2003, the Melanesian Brotherhood, an Anglican religious order, worked as mediators between armed groups. Seven brothers were murdered in 2003 while attempting to negotiate, and they are commemorated among the martyrs of the twentieth century at Westminster Abbey.

## Key Facts

- **Persecution Score:** Lower (5/100), **Rank:** #152 of 192 countries
- **Estimated Christian Population:** over 95 percent
- **Current Status:** Full religious freedom
- **Historical Note:** Seven Melanesian Brothers killed while mediating in 2003
